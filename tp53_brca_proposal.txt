PART 1: Comprehensive State-of-the-Art Report
1a. GRN Inference Methods: Capabilities, Directionality, and Fundamental Limitations
Gene Regulatory Network (GRN) inference is the computational problem of reverse-engineering the directed topology of transcriptional regulation from molecular measurements. The field has evolved rapidly from bulk statistical models to single-cell multi-omics frameworks, yet fundamental limitations in capturing temporal causal structure persist across all major paradigms. In the context of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and basal-like breast carcinoma — tumor subtypes defined by >80% TP53 mutation frequency — these limitations are particularly consequential.

SCENIC and pySCENIC
SCENIC operates through a three-step pipeline: (i) inference of co-expression modules between transcription factors (TFs) and candidate target genes via gradient-boosted regression (GRNBoost2); (ii) pruning of indirect targets using cis-regulatory motif enrichment (RcisTarget); (iii) per-cell regulon activity scoring via AUCell. Directionality is imposed by construction as TF to target, but the initial inference is purely associative, based on co-variation and predictive importance rather than causal intervention. The sign of regulation (activation versus repression) is not determined without additional assumptions. SCENIC produces a single static network per dataset: it does not estimate how regulatory edge weights evolve over time or across a continuous disease trajectory. For a project whose central hypothesis concerns TP53-driven network rewiring in TNBC, where rapid transition to drug-resistant attractor states is the dominant clinical challenge, SCENIC provides a useful regulon atlas but cannot model the law of state transition.

GRNBoost2
GRNBoost2 trains an independent gradient-boosted regression model for each target gene, using the expression of a candidate TF set as predictors. Edge weights are derived from feature importances, yielding a directed TF-to-gene network ranked by predictive association. The fundamental limitation is that predictive association is not causal intervention: indirect effects, feedback loops, confounders, and dropout artifacts all contaminate the inferred network. In TNBC single-cell data, where extreme intratumoral heterogeneity produces complex co-expression patterns, GRNBoost2 generates spurious or unstable edges without integration of prior regulatory evidence.

PANDA
PANDA (Passing Attributes between Networks for Data Assimilation) is an integrative message-passing framework that initializes a TF-to-gene network from motif priors, incorporates protein-protein interaction (PPI) data between TFs, and iteratively updates edge weights until a consensus condition-specific regulatory matrix is reached. PANDA can construct condition-specific networks for mutant versus wild-type samples and compare them across BRCA subtypes, but it cannot estimate the continuous trajectory of rewiring driven by a specific allele, nor can it distinguish the direct effect of the mutant protein from secondary downstream adaptation. In TNBC, where adaptation to chemotherapy occurs over days to weeks, this continuous temporal dimension is clinically essential.

VIPER
VIPER infers the activity of transcriptional regulators from gene expression signatures using pre-computed regulon networks, providing a sensitive readout of TF activation states even when the TF transcript itself is lowly expressed or subject to dropout. VIPER is not an edge-level GRN inference method but a protein activity inference layer. Its value for this project is as a validation target: the mutation-conditioned GNN model should produce predictions that correlate with VIPER-derived p53 activity scores across TCGA BRCA cohorts.

CellOracle
CellOracle reconstructs GRNs from single-cell multi-omics data by training regularized regression models constrained by chromatin accessibility evidence (TF motif binding within open ATAC-seq peaks), which grounds regulatory edges in physical binding plausibility. Its key innovation is the capacity for in silico TF perturbation. The critical limitation for a TP53 allele-specific project is that the mutation is not a structural input variable: CellOracle perturbs TFs generically and cannot distinguish the GRN consequence of R175H from R273H at a fixed TNBC cellular context, because the allele-specific gain-of-function rewiring of the chromatin landscape — particularly relevant in basal-like tumors with their extensively remodeled enhancer landscapes — is not encoded in the model.

Dictys
Dictys is the most methodologically advanced current tool for dynamic GRN reconstruction, explicitly designed to produce time-resolved, context-specific regulatory networks from paired scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq data. Dictys represents the closest existing baseline to the proposed project. However, even Dictys does not encode the mutation as a causal input variable: it reconstructs the network that best explains the observed data along a trajectory, but it cannot parameterize the function mapping a specific TP53 allele to a specific pattern of rewiring in a generalizable, transferable way across the clinical heterogeneity of TNBC.

Core limitation shared by all existing methods: none encodes the specific TP53 allele (e.g., R175H, R273H, R248W) as a structural causal input that modulates network parameters. They describe networks associated with mutant versus wild-type states, but they cannot learn a generalizable function mapping allele identity to topological rewiring trajectory. This limitation is especially acute for breast cancer, where allele-specific divergence in response to platinum-based chemotherapy, PARP inhibitors, and immunotherapy is clinically documented but mechanistically unexplained.

1b. Dynamic GRN Modeling: Definitions, Frameworks, and State of the Art
The concept of dynamics in GRN literature is not uniformly defined. Three major frameworks coexist, each capturing a different facet of biological regulatory dynamics.

Boolean Networks and Logical Models
Boolean networks represent gene expression states as binary variables and update them through logical regulatory rules. Their primary value lies in attractor analysis: steady states of the Boolean system correspond to stable cellular phenotypes, and the transition between attractors models cell fate changes. The EMT circuit — centrally relevant to TNBC metastatic competence — has been formalized as a Boolean system exhibiting multistability, with epithelial, mesenchymal, and hybrid attractor states. The hybrid EMT state is the most metastatically potent and is enriched in TNBC patient-derived xenografts. Andrea Tangherloni has developed forest-structured evolutionary algorithms for Boolean GRN reconstruction that significantly improve computational efficiency and precision in this framework.

ODE and SDE Models
Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) models define regulatory dynamics through continuous rate equations for molecular concentrations, capturing transcription, translation, and degradation with kinetic precision. The fundamental barrier is parameter identification: whole-genome ODE models require thousands of kinetic constants not empirically accessible at the scale of the TNBC GRN. Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) add molecular noise, capturing transcriptional bursting relevant to the extreme phenotypic plasticity of basal-like breast cancer cells, but at substantially higher computational cost.

Data-Driven and Single-Cell Trajectory Approaches
The dominant operational definition of dynamics in single-cell genomics is pseudotime: cells are ordered along an inferred progression axis based on transcriptomic similarity, and regulatory changes along this axis are interpreted as temporal dynamics. RNA velocity provides a complementary signal by estimating the time derivative of spliced mRNA abundance, yielding a vector field over the transcriptomic manifold indicating the direction of immediate cell fate change. In TNBC, multiple publicly available scRNA-seq datasets (GEO: GSE176078, GSE158508) provide high-quality trajectory data for drug-naive and chemotherapy-exposed tumor cells, making these approaches directly applicable to the proposed project.

The state of the art in modeling GRN state transitions: EMT and drug resistance in TNBC are increasingly formalized as transitions between attractor basins in a Waddington epigenetic landscape. Mutant TP53 GOF mechanisms lower the energy barriers between these basins, conferring the extreme plasticity that defines the basal-like phenotype. No current computational framework integrates this attractor-based dynamical systems view with allele-specific GRN rewiring learned from multi-omics data.

1c. GNN Architectures for Biological Networks
Graph Attention Networks (GATs) introduce a learned self-attention mechanism over the neighborhood of each node during message passing, assigning importance weights to neighbor contributions. The biological motivation is direct: in a regulatory network, not all regulators contribute equally to the expression of a target gene. GATs form the interpretable backbone of GENELink for regulatory link prediction from scRNA-seq data. A key limitation is that standard GATs assume homophily: connected nodes are expected to have similar feature representations. In a GRN, this assumption fails catastrophically for repressor TFs.

The Heterophily Problem in GRNs and Sheaf Neural Networks
In gene regulatory networks, transcriptional repressors are strongly anti-correlated with their targets: a maximally active repressor TF will drive its target toward minimal expression. This problem is particularly acute in TNBC, where the loss of canonical TP53 tumor suppressor activity co-occurs with gained repressive activity over differentiation and apoptotic programs through GOF protein-protein interactions. Standard GNNs that aggregate neighbor features via weighted averaging will blur the repressor-target distinction, degrading representational quality precisely at the most biologically important interaction class. Sheaf Neural Networks, recently developed by Pietro Lio's group at Cambridge, provide a rigorous topological solution to heterophily. Rather than aggregating raw node features, sheaf NNs attach mathematical vector spaces (sections of a cellular sheaf) to both nodes and edges, and learn linear maps (restriction maps) that transform node features into a shared edge space before aggregation. The resulting connection Laplacian can natively encode anti-correlated relationships, modeling transcriptional repression as a sign inversion in the restriction map.

Temporal GNNs (EvolveGCN, TGN) are the natural architectural class for modeling GRN rewiring as a dynamical process. These architectures were developed primarily for social and financial graphs; their application to biological multi-omics requires specific inductive biases they currently lack: integration of 3D chromatin constraints, handling of regulatory heterophily, and conditioning on genomic mutation status.

1d. TP53 Biology and Mutational Landscape
TP53 encodes a tetrameric transcription factor that functions as the primary guardian of genomic integrity, orchestrating transcriptional programs governing cell cycle arrest, DNA damage repair, apoptosis, senescence, and metabolic reprogramming. TP53 is mutated in approximately 35% of all new cancer diagnoses when epidemiologically weighted by incidence. Critically, in basal-like breast carcinoma and triple-negative breast cancer — the primary modeling system of this project — TP53 mutation frequency exceeds 80%, making it the defining molecular lesion of this subtype. This exceptional mutation frequency, combined with the aggressive clinical course and near-universal chemotherapy exposure of TNBC patients, makes breast cancer the optimal biological system for studying TP53 GOF-driven GRN rewiring toward drug resistance.

Structural Classes of Hotspot Mutations and Their Mechanistic Consequences
The mutational spectrum of TP53 is not uniform, and this non-uniformity is mechanistically critical for the proposed project. Two structural classes of hotspot mutations must be distinguished:
Contact mutants (R273H, R248W, R248Q): These mutations affect residues that directly contact the DNA major groove. The global protein fold is largely preserved, but DNA sequence specificity is radically altered. Contact mutants can bind to non-canonical DNA sequences and engage novel promoters, driving the transcription of oncogenes not targeted by wild-type p53. Their GOF activity is mediated primarily through aberrant transcriptional activation at new genomic loci. In TNBC, R273H has been associated with enhanced invasive capacity and resistance to doxorubicin through upregulation of the EGFR-MAPK axis.
Conformational mutants (R175H, G245S, R282W): These mutations cause partial or severe unfolding of the DNA-binding domain, exposing hydrophobic regions normally buried in the protein core. This structural exposure facilitates promiscuous protein-protein interactions with components of chromatin remodeling complexes and other nuclear regulators. R175H, the most common TP53 mutation in human cancer and enriched in TNBC, achieves GOF activity predominantly through protein-protein interactions rather than direct DNA binding, making it particularly suited to driving global chromatin reorganization.

GOF Mechanisms: Chromatin Remodeling and the Epigenetic Dimension
A mechanistically decisive body of evidence demonstrates that GOF mutant p53 isoforms actively recruit master chromatin remodelers to novel genomic loci, fundamentally reshaping the enhancer landscape of cancer cells. Specific mutants (R273H, R248W, and R175H) recruit the SWI/SNF complex, including the SMARCA4 ATPase subunit, and the Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2) methyltransferase EZH2, to previously inaccessible chromatin regions. This recruitment deposits activating histone marks (H3K27ac) at oncogenic enhancers and repressive marks (H3K27me3) at differentiation-associated gene loci. In TNBC, this mechanism explains the paradoxical observation that TNBC cells, despite lacking hormone receptors and HER2 amplification, maintain highly active transcriptional programs through GOF mutant p53-orchestrated enhancer remodeling. Furthermore, mutant p53 sequesters its family members p63 and p73, inhibiting their anti-metastatic transcriptional programs — a mechanism of particular relevance in basal-like breast cancer, where p63 normally maintains epithelial identity and where its sequestration contributes to the hybrid EMT state characteristic of metastatic TNBC.

Co-mutation Patterns in Breast Cancer and TNBC Subtype Specificity
The regulatory consequences of TP53 mutation are not cell-autonomous: they interact with the broader genomic context established by co-occurring driver mutations. In TNBC, TP53 mutations co-occur with amplifications at 8q24 (MYC), loss of RB1, BRCA1/2 germline and somatic alterations, PIK3CA mutations in a subset, and PTEN loss. The co-occurrence with MYC amplification is particularly consequential: both GOF mutant p53 and MYC recruit BRD4 and Mediator to super-enhancers, creating synthetic convergence at transcriptional amplifier loci. In BRCA1-mutant TNBC, TP53 mutations co-occur with genomic instability signatures (SBS3), and the allele-specific GRN rewiring must be interpreted in the context of homologous recombination deficiency. MDM2 amplification and TP53 mutation tend toward mutual exclusivity across tumor types, reflecting alternative mechanisms of p53 pathway inactivation. These co-mutation patterns define boundary conditions on the GRN topology that the proposed model must account for as contextual inputs.

1e. Tumor Evolution and GRN Rewiring in Breast Cancer
Cancer is an evolutionary process governed by the sequential accumulation of somatic mutations under selective pressure. In TNBC, the clinical evolutionary problem is starkly defined: the majority of patients respond to initial anthracycline/taxane chemotherapy, but resistant disease emerges in a substantial fraction within 18 months, and this drug-tolerant persister state is the proximate cause of cancer-related mortality. Crucially, in TNBC, emergence of drug resistance frequently occurs without acquisition of new genetic mutations, implicating epigenetic GRN reprogramming — driven by GOF mutant p53 — as the primary mechanistic engine.

Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is the canonical example of GRN-mediated cancer cell plasticity in TNBC. The basal-like subtype is defined by a hybrid EMT state, intermediate between epithelial and mesenchymal, which is the most metastatically competent and therapeutically resistant. These intermediate states are mathematically modeled as metastable attractors in the GRN state space. TP53 GOF mutations lower the energy barriers between these attractor basins by rewiring the enhancer landscape, conferring the cellular plasticity required for transition from drug-sensitive to drug-tolerant states. This is the precise biological mechanism that the proposed Temporal Sheaf GNN is designed to model.

1f. Multi-Omics Integration for GRN Reconstruction
Transcriptomic data alone (scRNA-seq) provides information on transcript abundance but cannot resolve whether a TF directly binds and regulates a target gene or acts through a cascade of secondary intermediaries. Chromatin accessibility data (scATAC-seq) provides physical evidence of regulatory potential by identifying nucleosome-free regions at promoters and distal enhancers, confirming that the transcriptional machinery has physical access to regulatory elements. Motif scanning algorithms applied to accessible peaks (using JASPAR 2024) identify putative TF binding sites, constraining the regulatory edge set to physically plausible interactions. Linking distal enhancers to their target gene promoters requires chromatin conformation data: Hi-C and HiChIP map the three-dimensional architecture of the genome, identifying Topologically Associating Domains (TADs) and specific promoter-enhancer loops. In TNBC cell lines (MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468, BT-549), extensive ENCODE Hi-C datasets are available, providing high-resolution chromatin conformation maps directly applicable to constraining the GRN graph construction with the enhancer landscape rewiring induced by GOF mutant p53.

PART 2: Critical Analysis and Identification of the Gap
The Exact Missing Piece
A rigorous synthesis of the state of the art reveals a single, precisely definable open problem at the intersection of GNN methodology and TP53 cancer biology. The field currently possesses three partially developed capabilities that, if unified, would constitute a transformative advance:
The ability to infer static or pseudo-dynamic GRN snapshots from single-cell multi-omics data (Dictys, CellOracle, SCENIC)
The ability to model dynamic network evolution with temporal GNN architectures (EvolveGCN, TGN)
Mechanistic knowledge that distinct TP53 hotspot mutations differentially recruit chromatin remodelers to rewire the enhancer landscape in an allele-specific manner, with particular relevance to TNBC biology

What is missing is a unified computational framework that:
Encodes the specific TP53 allele as a structural causal input variable that modulates GRN topology and dynamics, rather than treating mutation status as a binary label applied post-hoc
Learns a generalizable, transferable function mapping allele identity and cellular context to continuous temporal evolution of GRN edge weights, rather than inferring condition-specific static networks
Natively handles the heterophily of transcriptional repression through sheaf-based geometric deep learning, rather than failing silently under homophily assumptions
Integrates 3D chromatin architecture (Hi-C loop constraints) as structural priors on the evolving graph, grounding the learned dynamics in physical genomic organization — particularly critical in TNBC where super-enhancer remodeling by GOF mutant p53 is the primary rewiring mechanism
Produces a decomposition of rewiring into a context background component and an allele-specific sparse perturbation propagating within a bounded topological neighborhood of the TP53 node, making the biological hypothesis of TP53 as a master regulatory hub directly falsifiable

Why Current Methods Fail
SCENIC and GRNBoost2: descriptive, associative, static; no causal allele mapping.
CellOracle and Dictys: dynamic and perturbable, but mutation is not a structural input; cannot distinguish R175H from R273H in matched TNBC cellular contexts.
GAT-based GNN classifiers: demonstrate predictive power of gene-gene graphs for mutation classification, but cannot identify rewiring mechanisms or connect to evolutionary trajectories.
EvolveGCN and TGN: capable of temporal graph evolution, but lack biological inductive biases for multi-omics, assume homophily (failing on repressor TFs), and have no mechanism for conditioning on genomic mutation identity.

The biological question at stake is not whether TP53 mutations alter gene expression in TNBC (this is established) but which specific regulatory edges are rewired, in which temporal order, and with what consequences for attractor structure as a function of the specific allele and the TNBC cellular context. This question has not been asked computationally because the required architectural fusion (temporal GNNs + sheaf geometry + mutation conditioning + multi-omics priors) does not exist as a unified framework.

PART 3: Formulation of the Research Question
Primary Research Question

Biological Question
Do distinct structural classes of TP53 hotspot mutations (specifically, the contact mutant R273H versus the conformational mutant R175H) induce mathematically distinguishable temporal trajectories of GRN rewiring through differential recruitment of chromatin remodeling complexes (SWI/SNF and PRC2/EZH2) in triple-negative/basal-like breast cancer cells, and do these divergent rewiring trajectories produce distinct attractor landscapes that explain divergent patterns of cellular plasticity, EMT progression, and anthracycline/taxane resistance acquisition in TNBC?

Computational Translation
Can a Temporal Sheaf Graph Neural Network (TS-GNN), which integrates time-series scRNA-seq, scATAC-seq, and Hi-C data from TNBC patient-derived samples and cell lines while natively resolving transcriptional heterophily through cellular sheaf structures, learn a generalizable, allele-conditioned function that maps a specific TP53 mutation embedding to the continuous temporal evolution of GRN edge weights, such that the model (i) predicts distinct rewiring trajectories for R273H versus R175H at matched TNBC cellular contexts and (ii) identifies the topological bottleneck states preceding stable drug-resistant attractor establishment?

Mechanistic Hypothesis
The contact mutant R273H preferentially increases edge weights between SWI/SNF-occupied distal enhancers and core EMT driver genes (ZEB1, VIM, CDH2), rapidly stabilizing a mesenchymal-like attractor state associated with enhanced invasive capacity and chemotherapy evasion. The conformational mutant R175H, through its exposed hydrophobic surface and promiscuous protein-protein interactions, induces topological pathways associated with metabolic reprogramming (HIF1A targets, LDHA, PDK1) and genomic instability (BRCA2, RAD51 pathway suppression), establishing a distinct attractor trajectory characterized by replication stress tolerance. In BRCA1-mutant TNBC background, R175H-driven RAD51 suppression synergizes with homologous recombination deficiency to create a specific vulnerabilty to PARP inhibitors that the TS-GNN should capture as a high-weight allele-specific edge perturbation. These allele-specific trajectories are not reducible to simple loss of p53 activity but require the GOF protein-protein interaction network to emerge.

Impact Statement
If confirmed, this framework transforms the binary classification of TP53 as mutant versus wild-type in TNBC into a continuous, mechanistically interpretable operator on regulatory network topology and dynamics. It enables: (i) allele-level stratification of TNBC patients for targeted therapeutic intervention — platinum compounds versus PARP inhibitors versus anti-EMT strategies; (ii) identification of transient, pre-resistant network states as precision therapeutic windows before clinical resistance emerges; (iii) prediction of which co-mutation contexts (BRCA1 loss, MYC amplification, RB1 deletion) accelerate or decelerate the rewiring trajectory toward drug resistance; and (iv) rational nomination of combination therapy targets based on the specific attractor landscape of each TP53 allele in TNBC.

Secondary Research Questions
Secondary Question 1: Co-mutation Topological Synergy in TNBC
How do co-occurring driver mutations characteristic of TNBC — MYC amplification at 8q24, RB1 deletion, BRCA1/2 alterations, PIK3CA mutations, PTEN loss — modulate the temporal GRN rewiring trajectory initiated by specific TP53 alleles? Do they act as boundary conditions that restrict or accelerate convergence toward the drug-resistant attractor, and can the TS-GNN learn these synergistic interaction terms from multi-omics co-mutation data? This question maps onto the second chapter of a PhD thesis and connects the allele-specific rewiring model to the broader landscape of TNBC evolutionary genetics.

Secondary Question 2: In Silico Attractor Perturbation and Therapeutic Target Discovery
Can in silico perturbations applied to the learned parameters of the TS-GNN (node knockout, edge weight zeroing) accurately predict the minimal intervention set required to force a TNBC cellular state out of a drug-resistant attractor basin and redirect the trajectory toward an apoptosis-competent state? Validation against DepMap CRISPR screen genetic dependencies in TNBC cell lines stratified by TP53 allele provides a biological ground truth. This question connects the computational model directly to therapeutic application in a clinical context where new combination strategies are urgently needed.

Secondary Question 3: Translational Biomarker Derivation for TNBC
Can the transient network states identified by the TS-GNN during the R273H and R175H rewiring trajectories in TNBC be used to derive a minimal transcriptomic signature that predicts impending chemotherapy resistance in clinical patient cohorts before resistance is phenotypically manifest? Validation against the ISPY2 trial dataset and TCGA BRCA longitudinal samples connects the mechanistic computational model to clinical translational impact and provides the narrative justification for publication in high-impact clinical oncology journals as a companion paper.

PART 4: Project Design
A concrete, feasible 6 to 12 month Master's thesis project is designed below as a proof-of-concept for the TS-GNN framework and as the foundation for the PhD research program, with breast cancer as the primary biological system.
4a. Data Sources
Primary Cancer Cohort: Breast Cancer (TNBC/Basal-like)
Breast cancer serves as the primary training and mechanistic discovery cohort, based on the exceptionally high TP53 mutation frequency (>80% in basal-like/TNBC), the clinical urgency of chemotherapy resistance, the availability of extensive paired multi-omics data, and the availability of isogenic cell line models with defined TP53 backgrounds.
TCGA BRCA: >1,000 samples with matched whole exome sequencing (WES), RNA-seq, and methylation data, stratified by TP53 allele identity. Provides population-scale statistical power for allele-specific transcriptional signature validation and cross-patient generalization testing.
GEO single-cell datasets: GSE176078 (scRNA-seq from TNBC patient-derived samples, Pal et al. 2021, >25,000 cells), GSE158508 (paired scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq from TNBC), providing the cellular resolution multi-omics data required for dynamic GRN reconstruction and pseudotime trajectory inference.
TNBC cell lines with isogenic TP53 backgrounds (wild-type, R175H, R273H): Available from ATCC and the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE). MDA-MB-231 (R280K GOF), MDA-MB-468 (R273H), BT-549 (R249S), and CRISPR-engineered isogenic MCF10A (R175H, R273H) lines enable controlled allele-specific experiments.
Longitudinal paired samples: GEO datasets from treatment-naive and anthracycline/taxane-exposed TNBC patients provide the empirical temporal signal of chemotherapy-driven network rewiring.

Generalization Test Cohort: Colorectal Cancer (CRC)
CRC serves as the zero-shot cross-cancer generalization test cohort. TP53 is mutated in approximately 60% of CRC cases, with a well-characterized co-mutation landscape (KRAS, APC, SMAD4). CRC cell lines with isogenic TP53 backgrounds (HCT116, HT29) are available from ATCC and CCLE. Cross-cancer generalization from TNBC to CRC — tissues with distinct cellular lineages and epigenetic backgrounds — constitutes a stringent test of whether the TS-GNN has learned a biologically meaningful allele-structure relationship rather than a TNBC-specific lookup table.

Multi-Omics Data Modalities
scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq (10x Genomics Multiome): GEO datasets GSE176078 and GSE158508 for TNBC primary cohort; GSE178341 and GSE200997 for CRC generalization cohort.
Bulk RNA-seq and WES from TCGA/GDC: Pan-cancer bulk transcriptomic data stratified by TP53 mutation type from TCGA BRCA and TCGA COAD/READ provides the statistical power to validate allele-specific transcriptional signatures at population scale.
Hi-C and HiChIP (ENCODE): High-resolution chromatin conformation data from TNBC cell lines (MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468, MCF7) and CRC cell lines (HCT116, HT29) provides the promoter-enhancer loop maps used to constrain the base GRN graph.
ChIP-seq for SWI/SNF (SMARCA4), EZH2, and H3K27ac (ENCODE): Allele-specific binding evidence for chromatin remodelers in TNBC and CRC cell lines, serving as biological ground truth for validating inferred high-weight edges in the TS-GNN model.
Mutation Data: Exact TP53 allele identity, co-mutation status, and copy number alterations from COSMIC v3 and cBioPortal. DepMap CRISPR screen data for genetic dependency validation of predicted therapeutic targets in TNBC-stratified lines.

4b. Computational Pipeline
Step 1: Multi-Omics Preprocessing and Quality Control
scRNA-seq matrices are processed through CellRanger, followed by ambient RNA correction (SoupX) and doublet removal (Scrublet). Normalization uses scran pooling-based size factor estimation. scATAC-seq peaks are called using MACS2 and linked to the scRNA-seq cells via the 10x Multiome barcode correspondence. Batch correction is applied using Harmony for cross-sample integration across multiple TNBC patient biopsies, with careful preservation of biological variance attributable to TP53 allele status. Feature selection prioritizes transcription factors and their known target genes from TargetGeneReg 2.0 and RegNetwork, supplemented by highly variable genes enriched in TNBC-specific EMT and drug resistance programs.

Step 2: Allele Annotation and Cellular Stratification
For patient-derived samples, TP53 allele identity is extracted from matched WES or annotated metadata from GEO/TCGA and assigned to cancer cell clusters identified through copy number aberration (CNA) inference from scRNA-seq (using inferCNV or CopyKAT to distinguish malignant from stromal cells and tumor-infiltrating immune cells, the latter being particularly abundant in immunogenic TNBC). For cell line experiments, allele status is defined by the known genotype. Cells are stratified into R175H, R273H, R248W, R280K, wild-type, and other allele categories. This allele annotation becomes the conditioning variable for the TS-GNN.

Step 3: Base GRN Graph Construction with Multi-Omics Constraints
The regulatory prior graph is constructed through successive layers of evidence: (i) TF motif scanning of scATAC-seq peaks using JASPAR 2024 motif library to identify putative TF binding sites within accessible chromatin; (ii) application of Hi-C loop constraints from ENCODE TNBC cell line data to link distal enhancer-associated peaks to their target promoters within the same topologically associating domain; (iii) integration of RegNetwork and TargetGeneReg 2.0 regulatory priors for TF-target pairs with experimental support. This produces a directed, weighted regulatory graph where edge weights are initialized by peak accessibility scores and motif affinity scores. The TP53 node is annotated with the specific allele embedding. PPI data from STRING is used to define co-regulatory relationships among TF nodes, including the known p53-p63-p73 family interaction network relevant to TNBC.

Step 4: Pseudotime Trajectory and Dynamic Graph Sequence Construction
Cellular trajectories from drug-sensitive to drug-resistant states (or from early to advanced TNBC) are inferred using Monocle 3 or Slingshot on the integrated scRNA-seq manifold. RNA velocity (scVelo, deep learning variant) provides a vector field over the manifold indicating the direction of immediate transcriptional change, used to orient pseudotime branches. The cells are binned along the pseudotime axis into K temporal windows (K = 10 to 20 depending on trajectory length). Within each window, Dictys is applied to infer a context-specific GRN snapshot, yielding a sequence of directed weighted graphs A(t1), A(t2), ..., A(tK) that constitute the temporal training signal for the TS-GNN.

Step 5: Temporal Sheaf GNN Training
The TS-GNN architecture is built in two fused components: a static sheaf layer and a temporal evolution module. The sheaf layer, adapting the Neural Sheaf Diffusion framework from Lio's group, attaches a dxd restriction map to each directed edge in the regulatory graph, learned to map node feature spaces into a shared edge space. The connection Laplacian derived from these restriction maps drives the sheaf diffusion that constitutes message passing, natively encoding anti-correlated (repressor-target) relationships without the homophily failure mode of standard GNNs. The temporal evolution module uses a GRU to evolve the restriction maps of the sheaf at each pseudotime step. The TP53 allele embedding is injected as a conditioning signal through a hypernetwork that produces a multiplicative modulation of the attention weights in the GAT sub-layers within the sheaf computation. Loss function: a combination of MSE on predicted transcript abundance at time t+1 (supervised by the observed scRNA-seq data), a topological preservation regularizer penalizing topologically implausible rewiring events relative to the multi-omics prior graph, and an auxiliary regulon activity prediction loss using VIPER-derived activity scores as weak supervision targets.

Step 6: Attractor Landscape Mapping and In Silico Perturbation
Once trained, the TS-GNN parameterizes a learned vector field over the GRN state space. Fixed-point analysis of this vector field identifies stable attractors (drug-resistant and drug-sensitive TNBC states) and the energy barriers between them as a function of the TP53 allele embedding. In silico knockouts are implemented by zeroing the edge weights associated with specific TF nodes and re-running trajectory prediction, enabling systematic virtual screening of therapeutic targets. Predictions are benchmarked against DepMap CRISPR screen data in TNBC cell lines stratified by TP53 allele.

4c. Novel Methodological Contribution
The specific architectural innovation that distinguishes this project is the Temporal Sheaf Graph Neural Network (TS-GNN), making three simultaneous advances:
Sheaf-based heterophily resolution: By attaching cellular sheaf structures with learned restriction maps to the regulatory graph, the TS-GNN natively models transcriptional repression as an anti-correlated relationship without the homophily failure mode of standard GNNs. This is a principled geometric solution drawing directly on Pietro Lio's foundational work on Neural Sheaf Diffusion.
Allele-conditioned temporal evolution: The TP53 mutation embedding modulates the GRU-evolved restriction maps through a hypernetwork, making the temporal trajectory of GRN rewiring an explicit function of the specific allele. This enables the model to learn and generalize allele-specific rewiring laws in TNBC rather than describing condition-specific static networks.
Propagation decomposition with biological falsifiability: The allele-specific rewiring is parameterized as a sparse perturbation initiating at the TP53 node and propagating through the TNBC GRN within a bounded k-hop neighborhood, with strength decaying as a learned function of topological distance. If the model requires global rewiring beyond k hops to achieve good predictions, the master regulator hypothesis is challenged — itself a scientifically valuable finding in the context of TNBC's extreme transcriptional heterogeneity.

4d. Validation Strategy
Computational Validation
Cross-cancer zero-shot generalization: Train exclusively on TNBC multi-omics data. Evaluate zero-shot prediction of GRN rewiring signatures and trajectory direction in CRC samples harboring identical TP53 alleles (R175H and R273H cohorts from TCGA COAD/READ). Metrics: Pearson correlation of predicted versus observed differential edge weights; AUC for trajectory state classification.
Cross-allele generalization: Train on R175H and R273H cohorts; evaluate prediction on R248W and R280K cohorts (both common in TNBC), testing whether the allele embedding generalizes beyond the training allele set. This is the critical test of whether the model has learned a biologically meaningful allele-structure relationship.
Benchmark against CellOracle and Dictys: Compare predicted perturbation responses against CellOracle predictions for the same perturbations in TNBC, demonstrating that the TS-GNN reproduces established results while additionally providing allele-specific differentiation that CellOracle cannot.

Biological Validation

Appendix: Glossary of Key Technical Terms
Attractor state: A stable configuration of a dynamical system toward which nearby states converge; in TNBC GRN biology, corresponds to a stable cellular phenotype such as a luminal progenitor state, drug-sensitive TNBC state, hybrid EMT mesenchymal state, or drug-tolerant persister state.
Cellular sheaf: A mathematical structure that assigns vector spaces to the nodes and edges of a graph and linear maps (restriction maps) encoding how node states relate to edge states; used in Sheaf Neural Networks to resolve heterophilic interactions including transcriptional repression.
Connection Laplacian: The matrix operator derived from a cellular sheaf that governs diffusion of information across the graph, generalizing the standard graph Laplacian to handle anti-correlated relationships between repressor TFs and their targets.
GOF (Gain-of-Function): Neomorphic oncogenic activities acquired by missense TP53 mutant proteins, including aberrant transcriptional activation at oncogenic enhancers, chromatin remodeling complex recruitment (SWI/SNF, PRC2), and sequestration of p63/p73 tumor suppressors. Particularly prevalent in TNBC at >80% mutation frequency.
Heterophily: The property of a graph in which connected nodes tend to have dissimilar features; the opposite of homophily. Standard GNNs assume homophily and fail under heterophily conditions, which are common in GRNs due to transcriptional repression and particularly relevant to the p63/p73 sequestration axis in TNBC.
Hypernetwork: A neural network that generates the parameters (weights) of another neural network as a function of a conditioning input; used here to make the TS-GNN parameters a function of the TP53 allele embedding, enabling allele-specific GRN trajectory modeling.
Pseudotime: A computational ordering of single cells along an inferred differentiation or progression axis based on transcriptomic similarity, used as a proxy for developmental or disease time. Applied here to order TNBC cells along drug-naive to drug-resistant trajectories.
RNA velocity: The time derivative of spliced mRNA abundance, estimated from the ratio of unspliced to spliced transcript counts in scRNA-seq data; provides a directional vector field over the transcriptomic manifold indicating the direction of immediate cell fate change. Used to orient TNBC pseudotime branches toward resistance.
Regulon: A transcription factor together with its set of directly regulated target genes, defined by co-expression evidence and cis-regulatory motif enrichment.
Restriction map: In the sheaf framework, a linear map assigned to a directed edge that transforms the feature space of a source node into the feature space of the edge; learned during training to encode the regulatory relationship type (activation or repression), including the sign-inverted repression characteristic of GOF mutant p53 on differentiation programs.
TAD (Topologically Associating Domain): A genomic region within which chromatin interactions are enriched relative to interactions across TAD boundaries; provides structural constraints on which distal enhancers (including GOF mutp53-remodeled super-enhancers in TNBC) can regulate which promoters.
TNBC (Triple-Negative Breast Cancer): Breast cancer subtype lacking estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2 amplification; defined by >80% TP53 mutation frequency, aggressive clinical course, and limited targeted therapy options. Primary biological system of this proposal.
TS-GNN (Temporal Sheaf Graph Neural Network): The novel architecture proposed in this project, fusing Neural Sheaf Diffusion (for heterophily resolution) with EvolveGCN-style recurrent parameter evolution (for temporal dynamics) and allele-conditioned hypernetwork modulation (for mutation-specific GRN learning). First applied here to TP53-driven GRN rewiring in TNBC.
